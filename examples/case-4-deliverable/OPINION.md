# Tax Opinion — Relocation of the Regional Sales Function from Singapore to Vietnam

Prepared for: Fictional Manufacturing Group Ltd (PRC parent)
Engagement scope version: 2
Opinion reference: FTA-2026-0842

## Executive Summary

We have been asked to opine on three matters arising from the group's proposal to
relocate its Southeast Asian sales function from FMG Asia Sales Pte Ltd (Singapore)
to a newly incorporated FMG Vietnam Trading Co Ltd (Vietnam): the defensibility of
the proposed limited-risk distributor arrangement, the permanent establishment
exposure of the PRC parent arising from the Vietnamese activities, and the
withholding treatment of dividends and royalties flowing from Vietnam to the PRC.

Our conclusions are that the distributor arrangement is supportable at the upper end
of the proposed margin range but not at the lower end on the current functional
profile; that permanent establishment exposure is manageable but is materially
increased by the proposed secondment pattern and should be restructured before the
effective date; and that treaty rates on both dividends and royalties should be
available, subject to substance conditions which the group does not presently meet
in full and which are capable of being met with the steps we set out.

Each conclusion is expressed below at a stated confidence level, with the adverse
authority identified rather than omitted.

## Facts and Assumptions

We have assumed the following, as instructed. If any of these differs, this opinion
does not stand and must be reissued.

1. Annual regional revenue attributable to the function being relocated is
   approximately USD 240 million, and the profile of that revenue does not change
   materially as a result of the relocation.
2. FMG Vietnam Trading Co Ltd will purchase finished goods from the PRC parent and
   resell to unrelated customers in the region, taking title and legal ownership.
3. The Vietnamese entity will not own, develop, or enhance the group's trademarks or
   product technology, and will not bear inventory obsolescence or credit risk beyond
   a stated cap.
4. Six PRC-resident personnel will be seconded to Vietnam, each spending a majority
   of their working time in Vietnam, and each remaining on the PRC parent's payroll
   with the cost recharged to the Vietnamese entity.
5. The proposed distributor margin is within a range of 2.5% to 4.0% of net revenue.
6. The relocation takes effect on the first day of the group's next financial year,
   and the Singapore entity is wound down thereafter under a separate workstream.

## Questions Presented

**Q1.** Whether the proposed limited-risk distributor arrangement between the PRC
parent and FMG Vietnam Trading Co Ltd is supportable under the arm's length
principle, and what contemporaneous documentation the group must maintain.

**Q2.** Whether the activities of FMG Vietnam Trading Co Ltd, and of PRC-resident
personnel travelling to or seconded into Vietnam, give rise to a permanent
establishment of the PRC parent in Vietnam.

**Q3.** The withholding treatment of dividends and of royalties payable from Vietnam
to the PRC parent, including entitlement to treaty rates and the substance conditions
attached to them.

### Analysis — Q1

The arm's length character of a limited-risk distributor arrangement is determined by
the functions performed, assets used, and risks assumed, and not by the label applied
to the entity. Chapter I of the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines requires the actual
conduct of the parties to be delineated before any pricing method is selected, and
Chapter III governs the comparability analysis that follows. Where the delineated
transaction shows an entity performing only routine marketing and distribution
functions, holding no non-routine intangibles, and bearing capped risk, a one-sided
method benchmarking the distributor's return is appropriate.

On the assumed facts the Vietnamese entity performs order fulfilment, local marketing
execution, and customer service, takes title to goods, and bears a capped credit
exposure. It does not develop, enhance, maintain, protect, or exploit the group's
intangibles within the meaning of Chapter VI, which keeps the residual return with
the PRC parent. That allocation is defensible.

The pricing is where the proposal is weak. A distributor of this profile, at this
revenue scale, in this region, would ordinarily be benchmarked to an operating margin
in the region of three and a half to five percent of net revenue on comparable sets
we have reviewed. A 2.5% return sits below the interquartile range we would expect to
produce and would in our view invite adjustment by the Vietnamese authority under the
related-party provisions of the Law on Corporate Income Tax and its implementing
circulars on related-party transactions, notably Decree No. 132 and the documentation
thresholds it imposes. A return at or near 4.0% is supportable on the same evidence.

Documentation must be contemporaneous, not retrospective. The group must prepare and
hold, before the first filing due date following the effective date, a local file
containing the functional analysis, the benchmarking study with its search strategy
and rejection reasons, and the intercompany agreement executed before the transactions
it governs. A benchmarking study prepared after an enquiry has opened carries very
little weight, and Paragraph 5.3 of the Guidelines is explicit that documentation is
expected to be in place when the return is filed.

**Conclusion (Q1):** The arrangement **should** be supportable at a distributor margin
at or near the upper end of the proposed range, and is **more likely than not** to be
adjusted at the lower end. We recommend pricing at 4.0% with an annual review.

### Analysis — Q2

Permanent establishment exposure arises here on two distinct bases, and they must be
analysed separately because the answers differ.

The first is the fixed place of business test in Article 5 of the PRC-Vietnam double
taxation agreement. The Vietnamese entity's own premises are not by themselves a
permanent establishment of the PRC parent: a subsidiary is not automatically a
permanent establishment of its parent, as Article 5 paragraph 7 makes clear. The
exposure arises only if the parent has premises at its disposal in Vietnam through
which it carries on its own business. On the assumed facts it does not, provided the
seconded personnel are genuinely integrated into the Vietnamese entity's operations
and not operating a separate parent-level function from those premises.

The second, and the material one, is the dependent agent test. Where a person acts on
behalf of the parent and habitually concludes contracts, or habitually plays the
principal role leading to the conclusion of contracts routinely concluded without
material modification by the parent, a permanent establishment arises. The 2023
Protocol to the agreement aligned this language with the broadened formulation, which
narrows the room previously available for arrangements in which local staff negotiate
substantially all terms and the parent signs.

The secondment pattern as proposed increases this risk rather than reducing it. Six
PRC-resident personnel remain on the parent's payroll, with cost recharged. If those
individuals are customer-facing and lead negotiation, a Vietnamese authority has a
straightforward argument that they act on behalf of their legal employer. The
recharge does not answer the point; it is evidence of the cost, not of the direction
of the activity.

We recommend that the seconded personnel be employed directly by the Vietnamese
entity for the duration of the assignment, that authority to negotiate commercial
terms be documented as residing with that entity, and that the parent's involvement
be confined to product and technical support that does not lead to contract
conclusion.

**Conclusion (Q2):** On the arrangement as proposed, a dependent agent permanent
establishment is **more likely than not** to be asserted. With the restructuring of
the secondment described above, we consider that no permanent establishment **should**
arise.

### Analysis — Q3

Dividends and royalties are governed by Article 10 and Article 12 of the PRC-Vietnam
agreement respectively. Both provide reduced rates against domestic Vietnamese
withholding, and both make entitlement conditional in ways that matter on these facts.

For dividends, the reduced rate under Article 10 is available where the recipient is
the beneficial owner and holds the requisite participation. The participation
threshold is met on the assumed shareholding. Beneficial ownership is the live issue:
the PRC parent must have the right to use and enjoy the dividend without a
contractual or practical obligation to pass it on. Where the parent is itself
substantially financed by an upstream entity with matching distribution obligations,
the analysis becomes materially harder, and the authority will look at the
substance of the recipient rather than its incorporation.

For royalties, Article 12 caps the rate on payments for the use of, or the right to
use, trademarks and technical know-how. Two points require attention. First, the
characterisation of mixed payments: where a single fee covers both the licence and
ongoing technical services, the services element may fall outside Article 12 and be
taxed instead as business profits or as technical service fees under domestic law,
with a different rate. The intercompany agreement should separate the two, priced
independently. Second, entitlement is subject to the anti-abuse rule introduced by
the 2023 Protocol, which denies benefits where obtaining them was one of the
principal purposes of the arrangement.

Substance is therefore the operative condition for both. The PRC parent must be able
to demonstrate decision-making capacity over the assets generating the income:
personnel, premises, and documented board-level decisions taken in the PRC. The group
does not presently maintain contemporaneous board minutes evidencing the exercise of
that decision-making in respect of the regional business, and we regard that gap as
the principal exposure to a denial of treaty benefits.

**Conclusion (Q3):** Treaty rates under Article 10 and Article 12 **should** be
available on both dividends and royalties, provided the substance steps set out above
are implemented before the effective date and the royalty is separated from the
technical service fee in the intercompany agreement.

## Conclusions

1. **Q1:** The limited-risk distributor arrangement should be supportable at a margin
   at or near 4.0% of net revenue, with contemporaneous documentation in place before
   the first filing due date. At 2.5% it is more likely than not to be adjusted.
2. **Q2:** As proposed, a dependent agent permanent establishment is more likely than
   not to be asserted. With the seconded personnel employed by the Vietnamese entity
   and contracting authority documented locally, no permanent establishment should
   arise.
3. **Q3:** Treaty rates should be available on dividends and royalties, conditional on
   the substance steps identified and on separating the royalty from the technical
   service element of the intercompany fee.

## Limitations and Reliance

This opinion is given on the assumed facts stated above and fails if those facts
differ. It addresses only the PRC and Vietnam, and expresses no view on customs
valuation, Singapore exit charges on the wind-down of the existing entity, or
individual income tax for seconded personnel, each of which is outside the engagement
scope.

It reflects the law and published guidance as at the date of signature. The 2023
Protocol has limited administrative practice behind it, and our confidence levels on
Q2 and Q3 reflect that uncertainty rather than any doubt about the text.
